# Invoice Balance Dispute: the sample's own arguments

## Claim (Valencia Industrial Supply, Inc.)

We, Valencia Industrial Supply, Inc., are asking for payment of the remaining balance on Invoice VP-INV-260210 for the February 2026 order placed by Monterrey Fleet Solutions, LLC.
The original invoice was $29,500. Monterrey paid $8,000, and we later issued a $1,500 credit, resulting in an account balance of $20,000 before any further adjustment for the disputed items.
Monterrey continues to dispute several parts of the invoice. The main issues are the product pricing, the four missing oil filters, the $500 expedited delivery/handling charge, and the $1,875 inventory-management service charge.
We believe the remaining balance should be determined by looking at the Supply & Services Agreement, the accepted Purchase Order, and the parties’ contemporaneous business records.

1. The prices in the accepted Purchase Order should apply
We recognize that our January 30 pricing communication contained lower prices for the products. Attachment R-7 shows that communication, and Attachment R-8 shows that Monterrey responded that those were the figures its team was working from.
Monterrey then issued Purchase Order MFS-PO-260201 on February 2, 2026. The Purchase Order listed the specific quantities and unit prices for the February order. We accepted the Purchase Order in writing on February 3. See Attachment R-9 and Attachment R-10.
The Supply & Services Agreement §2.2 provides that a Purchase Order becomes binding when we accept it in writing, confirm it, begin performance, or ship the Products. The Agreement's §2.4 also provides that changes to the prices, quantities, delivery requirements, or Services in an accepted Purchase Order must be agreed to by both parties in writing.
The pricing provisions point in the same direction. Under Supply & Services Agreement §§3.1–3.2, the prices stated in the applicable accepted Purchase Order apply unless the parties expressly agree otherwise in writing. Section 3.3 requires a written pricing adjustment accepted by both parties before a different price applies.
The Agreement's §15.2 also states that the Agreement, accepted Purchase Orders, written pricing schedules, and written amendments make up the parties' agreement concerning the subject matter and supersede prior or contemporaneous proposals and understandings.
The January 30 pricing is therefore relevant background, but the February 2 Purchase Order is the later, specific written document for this particular transaction, and we expressly accepted it.
There is no later written amendment changing the PO prices.
For that reason, we believe the product charges should be calculated using the prices stated in Purchase Order MFS-PO-260201.

2. The four-filter discrepancy should not automatically result in another $168 credit
Our warehouse record shows that 100 oil-filter assemblies were packed for the shipment. Attachment R-15.
Monterrey's receiving records show that 96 were counted when the shipment arrived, creating a four-unit discrepancy. Attachment C-4, Attachment R-1, and Attachment R-16.
The Agreement specifically addresses this type of situation. Supply & Services Agreement §4.2 recognizes packing, shipment and receiving records. §4.3 requires a customer to report an apparent shortage within 10 business days, and §4.6 states that disputed delivery records should be evaluated using the parties' contemporaneous delivery, receiving, return and other business records.
The current records establish that there was a four-unit difference, but they do not establish what happened to those four filters after our packing count. The carrier proof of delivery confirms delivery to Monterrey, but it does not give a product-by-product quantity count. Attachment R-14.
We did not ignore the discrepancy. We issued Credit Memo VP-CM-260305 for $1,500 as part of the quantity and billing review. Attachment C-7 and Attachment C-13.
Our accounting record also shows that the $1,500 credit was posted as a quantity/billing adjustment and was not separately allocated to the $500 delivery charge or the $1,875 inventory-management service. Attachment C-16.
Under Supply & Services Agreement §§5.4–5.6, credits or adjustments for missing Products are based on what the business records establish, and disputed credit amounts are to be reviewed using the parties' contemporaneous records.
We therefore do not believe another $168 should simply be deducted by multiplying four filters by the PO price. The existing $1,500 credit should first be considered as part of the quantity and billing reconciliation.

3. The $500 expedited delivery charge should be considered in light of Monterrey's requested delivery
Monterrey requested delivery to its Henderson facility by February 9, 2026, explaining that the date was important for scheduled fleet-maintenance work. Attachment R-11.
We responded that we could target February 9. We also stated that if logistics identified a separate charge required to meet the requested timing, the amount and basis would be provided for review. Attachment R-12.
We then arranged priority handling and shipped the order on February 8. Attachment R-13. The carrier record shows delivery to Monterrey on February 9. Attachment R-14.
Invoice VP-INV-260210 separately identifies a $500 expedited delivery/handling charge. Attachment C-5.
Under Supply & Services Agreement §3.4, reasonable shipping or delivery charges may be separately identified on the invoice. Section 3.5 provides that charges must be authorized by the Agreement, an accepted Purchase Order, or a subsequent written agreement.
We recognize that the current record does not contain a signed document stating that Monterrey approved exactly $500. At the same time, the records show that Monterrey requested the time-sensitive delivery, we accommodated that request through priority handling, and the resulting charge was separately identified on the invoice.
We therefore ask that the arbitrator consider the delivery request, the priority handling, and the surrounding communications when deciding whether the $500 charge was properly payable.

4. The $1,875 inventory-management service was part of the accepted PO and was performed
The $1,875 inventory-management service was expressly included in Purchase Order MFS-PO-260201 and confirmed in our order confirmation. Attachment C-2 and Attachment C-3.
Under Supply & Services Agreement §§6.1–6.2, inventory-management services can be provided when identified in an accepted Purchase Order, and the supplier can charge for them when the applicable PO or other written agreement identifies the service and rate. Section 6.3 requires records showing the services performed.
Our service report, Attachment C-15, shows that pre-receipt inventory setup was completed, receiving reconciliation was performed, and the final variance review was started.
Monterrey's own service log, Attachment R-2, records substantially the same operational sequence. It also shows that the setup was completed and that receiving reconciliation was performed, although the final variance review remained incomplete.
The issue is therefore not whether we performed any inventory-management work. The records show that work was performed.
Supply & Services Agreement §6.5 provides that a dispute about an inventory-management service charge should be considered using the Purchase Order, service records, inventory reports, correspondence, and other contemporaneous business records.
We believe the evidence supports the $1,875 charge because the service was expressly authorized and work was performed. If an adjustment is considered appropriate because the final review was not completed, we believe that adjustment should reflect the portion of the work that was actually incomplete rather than removing the entire $1,875 charge.

5. We participated in the agreed invoice-dispute process
Monterrey raised its concerns about pricing, quantity, the delivery charge, and the inventory-management service shortly after receiving the invoice. Attachment C-9 through Attachment C-14.
The Supply & Services Agreement §§9.1–9.5 establishes the process for handling invoice disputes. The customer identifies the disputed charges, the parties review the relevant records, the supplier responds, and the parties attempt to resolve the issue in good faith.
We participated in that process. We provided shipment and service records, investigated the quantity issue, and issued the $1,500 credit. Attachment C-12, Attachment C-13, Attachment C-15, and Attachment C-16.
The Agreement's §9.7 also provides that participation in the dispute process, including partial payment or issuance of a credit, does not by itself waive either party's substantive rights concerning the remaining disputed amount.

6. The payment and credit result in a current balance of $20,000
The original invoice was $29,500. Attachment C-5.
Monterrey made an $8,000 payment on February 24. Attachment C-6.
We issued the $1,500 credit on March 5. Attachment C-7.
The current account balance is therefore:
$29,500 − $8,000 − $1,500 = $20,000.
This is the account balance before any additional adjustment that may be determined for the disputed items.
The Agreement recognizes electronic billing and payment-platform records as business records concerning invoices and payments. See Supply & Services Agreement §§7.2, 7.5–7.6 and §8.4.

7. Amount requested
We request payment of the $20,000 current account balance, subject to any specific adjustment that the arbitrator determines is supported by the evidence.
We also request any interest properly available under Supply & Services Agreement §8.5 on amounts determined to be properly due and unpaid after the applicable payment date.

Conclusion
We entered into a Supply & Services Agreement with Monterrey and then completed a specific February order through Purchase Order MFS-PO-260201.
The Purchase Order was accepted, the shipment was made, the order was delivered, the invoice was issued, Monterrey paid $8,000, and we issued a $1,500 credit.
The remaining questions concern particular parts of the account: the applicable product prices, the four-filter discrepancy, the $500 delivery charge, and the extent of the inventory-management service performed.
We believe the accepted Purchase Order and the parties' contemporaneous business records support payment of the remaining balance, subject to any adjustment that the arbitrator determines is supported by those records.

## Answer (Monterrey Fleet Solutions, LLC)

We, Monterrey Fleet Solutions, LLC, dispute Valencia Industrial Supply, Inc.’s request for the full $20,000 remaining on Invoice VP-INV-260210.
We do not dispute that we placed the February order, that the shipment was delivered, or that we owe an amount for products and services that were properly supplied and charged. Our disagreement is about how much is actually payable after the pricing, quantity, delivery-charge, service, and credit issues are considered.

1. The January 30 pricing should be considered when determining the correct product prices

Valencia sent pricing information on January 30 showing lower prices for the five product categories. Attachment R-7.
We responded that those were the figures our team was working from when preparing the February order. Attachment R-8.
We then issued Purchase Order MFS-PO-260201 on February 2 with higher product prices. Attachment R-9. Valencia accepted the Purchase Order on February 3. Attachment R-10.
We recognize that the accepted Purchase Order is important. However, the earlier pricing communication is still part of the history of the transaction and shows what pricing had been communicated and relied upon before the PO was issued.
The difference between the January 30 product prices and the later PO prices is $4,725.
Under Supply & Services Agreement §3.1, agreed prices may be stated in an accepted Purchase Order, quotation, or pricing schedule agreed to in writing. Section 3.2 provides that the unit prices in an accepted Purchase Order apply unless the parties expressly agree otherwise in writing. Section 3.3 addresses written pricing adjustments accepted by both parties.
The Agreement's §15.2 also addresses the relationship between the Agreement, accepted Purchase Orders, written pricing schedules, and prior proposals or understandings.
We therefore ask that the arbitrator consider Attachment R-7, Attachment R-8, Attachment R-9, and Attachment R-10, together with Supply & Services Agreement §§3.1–3.3 and §15.2, when determining the correct pricing for this order.

2. Four oil filters were not received
Valencia's packing record states that 100 oil-filter assemblies were packed before the shipment left Valencia. Attachment R-15.
Our receiving records state that 96 oil-filter assemblies were counted at delivery. Attachment C-4, Attachment R-1, and Attachment R-16.
We then checked the receiving area and did not locate the four missing filters. Attachment R-16.
The carrier proof of delivery confirms delivery to our Henderson facility, but it does not provide a product-by-product quantity count. Attachment R-14.
The result is a genuine four-unit discrepancy. The records do not establish that we actually received all 100 filters.
The Supply & Services Agreement §4.2 recognizes packing, shipment, delivery, and receiving records. Section 4.3 requires notice of an apparent shortage within 10 business days, and §4.6 provides that disputed delivery records should be evaluated using the parties' contemporaneous records.
The Agreement also provides in §5.4 that an appropriate credit or adjustment should be made where the available business records establish that products were missing. Section 5.6 provides a process for disputed credit amounts.
Valencia issued a $1,500 credit, but the credit record does not specifically state that it covered all four missing filters. Attachment C-7, Attachment C-13, and Attachment C-16.
We therefore ask that the four-filter discrepancy be considered when determining the final balance. If the arbitrator finds that the $1,500 credit already covered the shortage, no additional $168 adjustment is necessary. If the arbitrator finds that the shortage was not covered, the four filters should be valued using the applicable PO price.
3. The $500 expedited delivery charge was not separately approved
We requested delivery by February 9, 2026 because of scheduled maintenance work at our Henderson facility. Attachment R-11.
We also specifically asked Valencia to identify any additional delivery or handling costs before shipment so that they could be reviewed. Attachment R-11.
Valencia responded that if logistics identified a separate charge needed to meet the requested timing, it would provide the amount and basis for review. Attachment R-12.
Valencia then arranged priority handling and shipped the order on February 8. Attachment R-13. The carrier record shows delivery on February 9. Attachment R-14.
The invoice later included a separate $500 expedited delivery/handling charge. Attachment C-5.
We agree that we requested the February 9 delivery and that priority handling was used. The issue is whether those facts also establish that we agreed to pay a specific $500 charge.
Under Supply & Services Agreement §2.4, a change to delivery requirements or other terms of an accepted Purchase Order must be agreed to by both parties in writing. Section 3.4 addresses separately identified shipping or delivery charges, while §3.5 provides that charges must be authorized by the Agreement, an accepted Purchase Order, or a subsequent written agreement.
The current record does not contain a written approval of the specific $500 amount. Attachment R-4.
For that reason, we ask that the $500 charge be removed unless Valencia can establish a contractual basis showing that the particular charge was authorized.

4. The $1,875 inventory-management service was not fully completed
The $1,875 inventory-management service was included in the accepted Purchase Order. Attachment C-2 and Attachment C-3.
We do not argue that no work was performed.
Valencia's own service record shows:
pre-receipt inventory setup was completed;
receiving reconciliation was only partially completed; and
the final cycle-count and variance review was still in progress. Attachment C-15.
Our own Attachment R-2 service log records substantially the same sequence.
Under Supply & Services Agreement §§6.1–6.2, the service was authorized because it was identified in the accepted Purchase Order. Section 6.3 requires records showing the services performed, and §6.5 provides that a service dispute should be considered using the Purchase Order, service records, inventory reports, correspondence, and other contemporaneous records.
The issue is therefore not whether Valencia did anything. The issue is whether the full $1,875 should be charged when the records show that the final variance review remained incomplete.
We ask that the arbitrator determine whether the incomplete portion requires a reduction of the $1,875 charge and, if so, the appropriate amount based on the work actually performed.

5. The $1,500 credit should be included in the final reconciliation
The original invoice was $29,500. Attachment C-5.
We paid $8,000 on February 24. Attachment C-6.
Valencia issued a $1,500 credit on March 5. Attachment C-7.
The resulting account balance shown in the records is therefore:
$29,500 − $8,000 − $1,500 = $20,000.
We accepted the $1,500 credit for reconciliation purposes but continued to dispute the remaining balance. Attachment C-14.
Under Supply & Services Agreement §§10.1–10.6, issued credits and additional adjustments are part of the account-reconciliation process, and the parties are to compare invoices, payment records, credit records, delivery records, service records, and other contemporaneous business records when determining the account balance.
The $1,500 credit therefore needs to be considered together with the other disputed items rather than treated as resolving every issue by itself.

6. We properly raised the invoice dispute
We disputed the invoice shortly after receiving it and identified the pricing, quantity, delivery charge, and inventory-management service as disputed items. Attachment C-9 and Attachment C-11.
The Supply & Services Agreement §9.1 requires invoice disputes to identify the invoice and disputed charges. §9.3 provides for review of supporting records, §9.4 requires a supplier response, and §9.5 calls for good-faith efforts to resolve the dispute.
We followed that process. We raised the issues, requested supporting records, and continued communicating with Valencia while the parties attempted to reconcile the account. Attachment C-9 through Attachment C-14.
The Agreement also provides in §8.2 that a good-faith dispute concerning part of an invoice does not excuse payment of the undisputed portion.
We made an $8,000 payment and continued disputing the amounts we believed required further review.

7. Current reconciliation
After the $8,000 payment and $1,500 credit, the current balance is $20,000.
Our current base reconciliation removes the $500 delivery charge and the full $1,875 service charge:
$20,000 − $500 − $1,875 = $17,625.
Attachment R-17.
We have not added another $168 deduction for the four filters in that base calculation because the existing $1,500 credit was posted as a quantity/billing adjustment and the credit record does not separately state that the four filters were excluded. Attachment C-16 and Attachment R-17.
If the arbitrator determines that the four-filter shortage was not covered by the $1,500 credit, our alternative calculation would apply an additional $168, based on four filters at the $42 PO price, resulting in $17,457.
The final amount may also change depending on how the January 30 pricing and February 2 Purchase Order are treated.

8. What we are asking for
We ask the arbitrator to determine the final balance after considering:
the January 30 pricing communication and the later Purchase Order;
the four-filter quantity discrepancy;
whether the $500 delivery charge was authorized;
the extent to which the $1,875 inventory-management service was completed; and
the $1,500 credit already issued.
We agree that an amount remains payable. We do not agree that the current $20,000 balance automatically represents the amount properly due.

Conclusion
This was a real commercial transaction, and we have already paid $8,000 toward the invoice and accepted the $1,500 credit.
The remaining disagreement is about the final amount.
The records show a pricing difference between the January 30 communication and the later Purchase Order, a four-unit oil-filter discrepancy, a $500 delivery charge that was not separately approved in writing in the current record, and inventory-management work that was not fully completed.
We therefore ask that the arbitrator apply the Supply & Services Agreement and the contemporaneous purchase-order, pricing, shipping, receiving, service, invoice, payment, credit, and reconciliation records to determine the amount that is properly payable.
