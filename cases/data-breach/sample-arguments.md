# Data Breach Dispute: the sample's own arguments

Reference only. These are the human-written arguments that ship with the DecisionLayer sample. caseagent never reads this file: every filing it produces is written by the agent from the dispute, the contract and the exhibits.

## Claim (Jane Doe)

WeMoveYou's "Changes to the Terms" clause creates an impermissible, illusory agreement. Because the text states changes are "effective immediately when we post them" and that users must check the page "from time to time," the Respondent claims the right to unilaterally alter the rules of engagement at any millisecond. Under New York law, a party cannot bind a consumer to a contract where they retain the unchecked power to retroactively alter or erase their own obligations at will; this lack of mutuality makes the contract (and its dispute clauses) unenforceable.

By incorporating the Privacy Policy by reference, the Respondent made a binding promise to protect data. The metric for "reasonable safeguards" must be measured against the statutory requirements of the NY SHIELD Act.

## Answer (WeMoveYou LLC)

Claimant's "illusory contract" argument is entirely irrelevant to this dispute. Even if the contract allows for future unilateral changes, the core provisions restricting liability—specifically the Disclaimer of Warranties (providing the site "as is") and the Limitation of Liability (capping damages)—were active on October 9, 2024, were active at the time of the breach, and have never been altered.

Because these specific liability shields remained static and unchanged throughout the parties' entire interaction, there is no retroactive manipulation. The consumer was on constant inquiry notice of these risk allocations, which strictly bar claims for consequential data breach damages or cap them at nominal amounts.

## Reply (Jane Doe)

The Limitation of Liability clause cannot be separated from the toxic "Changes" clause. Because the Respondent reserves the right to modify any part of the terms at any time without direct notice, the entire contract is infected with a lack of consideration.

Furthermore, a static Limitation of Liability clause cannot be used as a shield against gross negligence or a total failure to maintain the data security standards legally mandated by the NY SHIELD Act. A business cannot contract out of its statutory duty to protect consumer data by simply burying an "as is" clause in an online scroll box.

## Response (WeMoveYou LLC)

Under standard commercial principles, individual risk-allocation clauses remain fully enforceable so long as the consumer manifested assent by using the service. The Claimant received the exact service she paid for; she cannot enjoy the benefits of the transaction and then claim the underlying contract never existed to escape the agreed-upon liability caps.

The disclaimers governing the website's functionality were prominent and explicit. The platform never guaranteed absolute immunity from third-party cybercriminals, and the contract explicitly allocated the risk of data anomalies or technical exploits to the user.
