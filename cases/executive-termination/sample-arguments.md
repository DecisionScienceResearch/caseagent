# Executive Termination Dispute: the sample's own arguments

Reference only. These are the human-written arguments that ship with the DecisionLayer sample. caseagent never reads this file: every filing it produces is written by the agent from the dispute, the contract and the exhibits.

## Claim (Bobson Dugnutt)

Mr. Dugnutt was a junior Vice President of Engineering at Technodyne since January of 2018.

Mr. Dugnutt alleges that TechnoDyne terminated his employment on March 5, 2026, without just cause. The stated reason—"misconduct reflecting poorly on the company"—refers to a private verbal argument Mr. Dugnutt had at a local grocery store while off-duty. Mr. Dugnutt contends that:

The incident involved no illegal activity.

He was not wearing company branding nor acting as a representative of TechnoDyne.

The clauses in his contract governing conduct are unconstitutionally vague and do not grant the employer total authority over his private life.

As Mr. Dugnutt was earning $170,000 a year, he is entitled to $85,000 in six (6) monthly payments and COBRA or reinstatement.

## Answer (Technodyne Inc.)

TechnoDyne maintains that the termination was lawful and constituted Cause under Section 6(a) and 6(b) of the employment agreement. While the incident occurred off-site, it was recorded and went viral on social media (3.2 million views), where Mr. Dugnutt was quickly identified as a "Senior Lead at TechnoDyne."

Reputational Damage: The video shows Mr. Dugnutt using aggressive language, which violates our Corporate Code of Conduct.

At-Will Status: Mr. Dugnutt’s contract explicitly states employment is "at-will," allowing termination for any non-discriminatory reason.

Brand Alignment: TechnoDyne's clients expressed concern over the association, making his continued employment a liability.

## Reply (Bobson Dugnutt)

The Respondent’s reliance on "viral" status is a reactive overreach. The video was heavily edited to omit the provocation Mr. Dugnutt faced. Furthermore:

Selective Enforcement: Other employees have had public disputes (e.g., the "Parking Lot Incident" of 2024) without termination. In that incident, an employee got into a heated argument with a parking attendant at our corporate headquarters.

Privacy Expectation: A grocery store is a non-professional setting. If TechnoDyne wishes to police 24/7 behavior, the contract must provide a "Continuity of Conduct" stipend, which it does not.

Lack of Due Process: Mr. Dugnutt was fired via email without an opportunity to provide the full context of the recording.

## Response (Technodyne Inc.)

Mr. Dugnutt's behavior has unambiguously brought negative attention to our company and as an executive that is unacceptable. We will not continue to employ him and, as an at will employee, we have no obligation to do so.
