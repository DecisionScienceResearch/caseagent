## Claim (Logistics Inc.)

I. Nature of the Action
This is a maritime claim for breach of contract and cargo damage arising from the ocean carriage of industrial machinery from the Port of Rotterdam to the Port of Savannah.

II. Statement of Facts
On January 15, 2026, Logistics Inc. delivered a shipment of precision CNC machinery to ShipCo in "good order and condition."

ShipCo issued a Clean Bill of Lading (B/L #12345), acknowledging receipt of the goods without exception. The bill of lading notes a value of $75,000 of the cargo.

Upon arrival in Savannah on February 5, 2026, the container seals were intact, but internal inspection revealed that the machinery had shifted and sustained heavy impact damage and saltwater corrosion.

III. Argument / Basis for Liability
Prima Facie Case: Under COGSA, 46 U.S.C. § 30701, Claimant establishes a prima facie case by showing the goods were delivered to the carrier in good condition and discharged in damaged condition.

Breach of Duty: ShipCo failed in its non-delegable duty to properly load, stow, and care for the cargo pursuant to 46 U.S.C. § 30704.

Unseaworthiness: The presence of saltwater indicates a breach in the hull or hatch covers, rendering the vessel unseaworthy for the intended voyage.

## Answer (ShipCorp)

I. General Denial
ShipCo denies any liability for the damage alleged by Logistics Inc. and asserts that it exercised due diligence at all times to ensure the vessel's seaworthiness.

II. Affirmative Defenses
Insufficiency of Packing (COGSA § 4(2)(n)): The damage was caused by the Claimant’s failure to properly brace and secure the machinery inside the container. The "shifting" mentioned by the Claimant is a direct result of internal packing failures, not vessel motion.

Perils of the Sea (COGSA § 4(2)(c)): During transit, the vessel encountered an unforecasted Force 10 storm. Any damage resulted from "Perils of the Sea" which exceed the standard expected of a seaworthy vessel.

Catch-All Exception (COGSA § 4(2)(q)): ShipCo asserts that the damage occurred without the actual fault or privity of the carrier or its agents.

III. Counter-Argument on Damages
Even if liability were found (which is denied), ShipCo’s liability is strictly limited to $500 per package under the COGSA "Package Limitation" ($500/unit), unless the Claimant declared a higher value on the Bill of Lading and paid an ad valorem freight rate, which they failed to do.
