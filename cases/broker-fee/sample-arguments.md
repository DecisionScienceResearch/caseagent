# Real Estate Broker Fee Dispute: the sample's own arguments

Reference only. These are the human-written arguments that ship with the DecisionLayer sample. caseagent never reads this file: every filing it produces is written by the agent from the dispute, the contract and the exhibits.

## Claim (John Q. Stevenson)

Under the preamble of our Agreement executed in January 2026, the Broker explicitly promised to 'assist BUYER throughout the transaction and act in BUYER's best interests at all times.' Greenacre completely failed this fiduciary duty.

Two weeks after moving in, structural engineers discovered a decommissioned, leaking underground oil tank in the backyard that has contaminated the local soil, resulting in an emergency environmental cleanup. Alina Glenn knew I was looking for a worry-free property. She failed to review public environmental registries or warn me about the potential for buried tanks in this historic neighborhood. Greenacre did not act in my best interests, yet they pocketed a massive 3% fee at closing. Because they breached their foundational core promise of representation, they are not entitled to retain that compensation. I paid $42,000 to remediate the tank - they should pay me the full amount. The remediation bill is attached.

## Answer (Greenacre Realty LLC, represented by Alina Glenn)

Greenacre Realty LLC requests an immediate summary dismissal of this claim based on the clear, explicit limitations of scope set forth in Section 2 (Advice on Technical Matters) of the executed contract.

Section 2 explicitly states that the Broker will not: 'research, give advice or opinion concerning zoning, building... affecting the property' nor 'search for, discover or give opinions regarding environmental conditions... including but not limited to... buried oil tanks.'

Furthermore, Section 2 placed the 'absolute obligation to obtain information concerning the condition of the property from sources other than the Broker' squarely onto Mr. Stevenson. We fulfilled our role by locating the property, negotiating terms, and bringing about a meeting of the minds under Section 1. We are contractually insulated from environmental liabilities that the buyer explicitly undertook to investigate himself.

## Reply (John Q. Stevenson)

The Broker cannot use the boilerplate exclusions of Section 2 to completely wipe out their overarching fiduciary duty to act in my 'best interests at all times.'

While Section 2 says they aren't required to search public records, the final sentence of that very same section states: 'the broker hereby agrees to truthfully and honestly disclose to the Client any and all information affecting both the property and the locale... of which the Broker has actual knowledge.'

Alina Glenn has closed three other homes on Hudson Way between 2024 and late 2025. She had 'actual knowledge' that buried oil tanks are a systemic issue in this specific development. By remaining silent and rushing the deal to close to secure her 3% commission, she violated the honesty mandate of Section 2. The boilerplate waiver is void where the broker possesses contextual actual knowledge and fails to disclose it

## Response (Greenacre Realty LLC, represented by Alina Glenn)

The Claimant is engaging in baseless speculation. Selling other homes on the same street does not equate to 'actual knowledge' of a specific, invisible underground tank at this distinct parcel. Section 2 explicitly warned the client: 'Under no circumstances is the Client entitled to rely on any statement or representation of the Broker with respect to environmental condition.'

Greenacre Realty honestly disclosed everything we actually knew about 88 Hudson Way—which was nothing regarding underground tanks. Finally, we note that under Section 10(d) (Limitation of Liability), even if liability were somehow found, our maximum exposure is capped at the aggregate amount received. However, because no breach of the actual knowledge standard occurred, we request a total defense victory and an award of our reasonable attorneys' fees and DecisionLayer filing costs under Section 10(c).
