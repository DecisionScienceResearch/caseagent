# Commercial Lease Commencement Dispute: the sample's own arguments

Reference only. These are the human-written arguments that ship with the DecisionLayer sample. caseagent never reads this file: every filing it produces is written by the agent from the dispute, the contract and the exhibits.

## Claim (BevMart Inc.)

Pursuant to Article II, Section 2 of the Lease Agreement, the 'Commencement Date' is strictly defined as 'the date on which the Tenant shall commence to conduct business on the Leased [premises].' Due to administrative delays by the City of New York in processing municipal liquor licenses, the Tenant could not legally or practically conduct business on the premises until July 1, 2022. Therefore, the rent trigger was not activated until July 1, 2022. The Landlord’s assessment of rent and late fees for May and June 2022 violates the express contractual definition of the Commencement Date. Furthermore, the clause containing the sixty (60) day parameter contains a material drafting error ('Leased remised'), rendering the backstop restriction ambiguous and unenforceable.

## Answer (Arcology LLC)

The Tenant misinterprets the conditional logic of Article II, Section 2. The clause explicitly states that the business commencement trigger applies 'so long as such date is not in excess of sixty (60) days subsequent to execution hereof.' The lease was executed on March 1, 2022. The 60-day hard cap expired on April 30, 2022. By operation of law and contract text, the Commencement Date automatically defaulted to April 30, 2022, regardless of the Tenant's third-party licensing hurdles. The typographical error ('remised' instead of 'premises') is a clear scrivener's error that does not defeat the obvious mutual intent of the parties to limit the Landlord's rent abatement exposure to a maximum of 60 days.

## Reply (BevMart Inc.)

The Landlord's assertion that the 60-day clause acts as an automatic default trigger is unsupported by the text. The phrase 'so long as' creates a condition precedent, not an automatic acceleration mechanism. If the condition is not met because business did not commence, the contract fails to provide an alternative default trigger date, creating a latent ambiguity. Because the Landlord drafted this agreement, any ambiguity—including the unintelligible phrase 'Leased remised'—must be construed strictly against the Landlord under the doctrine of contra proferentem. The Tenant cannot be penalized for failing to operate when doing so would violate municipal law.

## Response (Arcology LLC)

The Tenant’s reliance on contra proferentem is inapplicable here, as both parties are sophisticated commercial entities represented by counsel. The phrase 'so long as' operates as a negative covenant and a limitation on the Tenant's benefit. To interpret the clause otherwise would allow the Tenant to indefinitely delay rent obligations by simply choosing not to open for business, destroying the economic substance of the transaction. A minor typographical error does not invalidate an explicit, bargained-for 60-day temporal backstop.
