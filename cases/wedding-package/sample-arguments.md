## Claim (Sophia Bennett)

I hired Hana Luxvita Weddings LLC for the $10,000 Hana Luxvita Signature Wedding Package and authorized a $3,000 payment-card transaction on January 15, 2026 toward that package. I am not disputing that I authorized the transaction. I am disputing Hana Luxvita's right to retain the full $3,000 charge because the planning and coordination services for which I paid were not provided at the level required by the agreement. The package included wedding planning and coordination, maintenance of the planning timeline, vendor communications and coordination, budget and logistical planning, development of the wedding-day schedule, rehearsal coordination, and wedding-day coordination. Those services were important reasons I chose the full-service package rather than handling the planning and coordination myself. [Attachment C-1; Attachment C-2; Attachment C-3]

I want to be fair about what Hana Luxvita did. They performed substantial work. They helped secure the photographer, florist, and DJ, communicated with the vendors, worked on the planning timeline, coordinated the rehearsal, and provided a coordinator on the wedding day. I am not claiming that Hana Luxvita did nothing, that the transaction was unauthorized, or that my wedding failed. My complaint is that the services ultimately provided did not fully match the full-service planning and coordination that I purchased and for which the disputed charge was made.

The agreement specifically identifies services that Hana Luxvita was responsible for providing, including maintaining the planning timeline, communicating with confirmed vendors, coordinating vendor timing and logistics, preparing the wedding-day schedule, coordinating the rehearsal, and providing wedding-day coordination. The agreement also provides that Hana Luxvita will use reasonable care and diligence in performing services it expressly agreed to provide. [Attachment C-3]

The first significant concerns came up in July. On July 8, I raised that I still did not have the finalized floral design and updated wedding-day timeline and asked Hana Luxvita to identify what remained outstanding and when those items would be completed. Hana Luxvita responded and later provided an updated working timeline on July 20. However, that working timeline still showed several important matters as pending, including the reception layout, floral approval, and final vendor timing. [Attachment C-4; Attachment R-5; Attachment R-7]

I understand that wedding planning develops over time and that some details depend on client decisions and vendor confirmations. I was not expecting every detail to be permanently finalized months before the wedding. My concern was that organizing, coordinating, and consolidating these details was itself part of the service I had hired Hana Luxvita to provide. I repeatedly had to ask for information to be brought together rather than receiving the level of consolidated coordination I expected from a full-service planning package.
The floral planning was another example. The preliminary floral proposal I received was materially different from what I understood had been discussed, including the ceremony arch, head-table flowers, and reception arrangements. I raised the issue with Hana Luxvita rather than simply treating it as a reason to cancel. Hana Luxvita worked with the florist and revised the proposal, which I appreciated. The revised proposal restored the main floral elements, but the particular blush garden rose I had wanted was ultimately unavailable and a premium blush spray rose was substituted. [Attachment C-6; Attachment R-8; Attachment R-9; Attachment R-10]

I recognize that the florist was an independent provider. I am not claiming that Hana Luxvita guaranteed the florist's inventory or should be responsible for every decision made by the florist. My concern is about Hana Luxvita's own responsibility to coordinate the vendors and handle the planning and logistics that were part of its package. The agreement expressly preserves Hana Luxvita's responsibility for coordination obligations that it undertook even where an independent provider performs the underlying service. [Attachment C-3]
I continued raising concerns about the overall schedule as the wedding approached. On August 12, I identified that photography arrival, floral installation, and DJ setup were still listed as targets rather than confirmed times; that the transition between the ceremony, photographs, and cocktail hour was not yet confirmed; that I still wanted the final floral design; and that the final schedule should include rehearsal timing, vendor contacts, and Hana Luxvita's wedding-day responsibilities. I also explained that my concern was the coordination function because I had hired Hana Luxvita so that I would not have to obtain and reconcile those details separately. [Attachment C-10]

Hana Luxvita subsequently obtained further vendor confirmations and continued developing the consolidated schedule. I acknowledge that improvement. The September records show that the planning became substantially more complete and that the major vendor timings and final floral arrangements were ultimately documented. [Attachment R-12; Attachment R-13; Attachment R-14; Attachment R-15]

But the fact that Hana Luxvita eventually completed or corrected items does not mean there was no earlier deficiency. The agreement itself provides an opportunity to review and correct reported service issues; it does not state that an attempt to correct an issue automatically eliminates the underlying deficiency. I raised the problems before the wedding and gave Hana Luxvita an opportunity to address them rather than immediately terminating the arrangement. [Attachment C-10; Attachment R-12; Attachment R-13; Attachment R-15]

I recognize that some final information and approvals were mine to provide. At the same time, Section 7.7 makes clear that a customer's issue does not automatically excuse an independent obligation that Hana Luxvita could still perform. [Attachment C-3]
The change of coordinator the night before the wedding was another concern. Hana Luxvita informed me that Maya Ellis, who had been handling my planning, could not attend because of an unexpected family emergency. I understand that the emergency itself was outside Hana Luxvita's control, and I am not blaming Hana Luxvita for the emergency. Hana Luxvita did arrange for Jordan Lee to take over and provided him with the planning file. [Attachment R-17]

My concern was the timing and the loss of continuity. Jordan was taking over immediately before the wedding after Maya had handled the planning process. I had to repeat some family-photo and floral information that I had already provided. Hana Luxvita later acknowledged that I had to repeat some details to Jordan. [Attachment R-17; Attachment R-19]

Jordan was professional, and I recognize that he ultimately carried out substantial wedding-day coordination. The October 17 record shows vendor arrivals, venue access, floral installation, photography, DJ setup, the ceremony, cocktail transition, reception, and the reception program. [Attachment R-18]

There was also a reception-transition issue in which I personally answered a question concerning the order of two reception events because the information was not immediately available to the coordinator. I recognize that this did not cause a major disruption and that the reception proceeded. I am not relying on that isolated incident as evidence that Hana Luxvita failed to coordinate the entire wedding. I identify it as another example of the coordination gap that I experienced.

The overall issue is therefore not whether Hana Luxvita performed some services. It clearly did. The question is whether Hana Luxvita provided the level of full-service planning and coordination for which I paid and whether it is entitled to retain the entire disputed $3,000 payment-card charge.

I understand that the agreement provides that a payment dispute does not automatically create a right to a refund and that service-performance and refund provisions must be considered. I am not asking for a refund simply because I was dissatisfied. I am asking the arbitrator to consider the actual services performed, the documented deficiencies, the corrective efforts, and the effect of those deficiencies on the value of the contracted coordination service. [Attachment C-3]

I also recognize that the agreement provides for consideration of whether a service was provided, whether it was materially different from the purchased service, the opportunity to address reported issues, and the parties' records concerning complaints and resolution. Those provisions are directly relevant to this dispute because my challenge concerns the merchant's entitlement to retain the charge for the services that were purchased. [Attachment C-3]

I initially attempted to resolve the dispute without arbitration. I requested $1,500 from the $3,000 payment and later offered to resolve the matter for $1,250. Hana Luxvita offered $500 and later increased that offer to $750. Those discussions did not resolve the dispute. [Attachment C-16; Attachment R-19; Attachment C-17; Attachment R-20]

I am now challenging the $3,000 card charge itself. I am not claiming that I paid $6,000, and I am not alleging that the transaction was unauthorized. My position is that, although the charge was authorized, the evidence does not support Hana Luxvita retaining the full amount because the contracted planning and coordination services were not fully delivered at the level promised.

I also recognize the substantial work Hana Luxvita performed. The planning progressed, vendors were coordinated, the rehearsal was completed, and the wedding itself proceeded successfully. Those facts should be considered when determining the amount of any remedy. I am asking the arbitrator to consider both sides of the record rather than treating the dispute as all-or-nothing.
For those reasons, I respectfully request that the arbitrator determine that Hana Luxvita is not entitled to retain the entire $3,000 disputed charge, and award the portion of that charge that the evidence establishes should be returned to me.

## Answer (Hana Luxvita Weddings LLC)

Hana Luxvita Weddings LLC respectfully disputes Sophia Bennett's challenge to the $3,000 credit-card charge associated with the Hana Luxvita Signature Wedding Package. The transaction was authorized by Sophia, and the evidence shows substantial performance of the services purchased.

Under Sections 14 and 15 of the agreement, the package included planning and coordination services such as maintaining the planning timeline, communicating with confirmed vendors, coordinating vendor timing and logistics, preparing the wedding-day schedule, coordinating the rehearsal, and providing wedding-day coordination. Hana Luxvita performed these services throughout the planning process and on the wedding day. [Attachment C-1; Attachment C-2; Attachment C-3]
Hana Luxvita does acknowledge that Sophia raised concerns during the planning process. Those concerns were not ignored. Under Sections 9 and 18, the agreement provides for customers to identify service concerns and for Hana Luxvita to review and address them. Hana Luxvita did so here, including by making further planning updates and continuing to work with Sophia before the wedding.
When Sophia reported concerns about the planning timeline, floral arrangements, and consolidated vendor information, Hana Luxvita responded and continued working with her. Hana Luxvita provided updated timelines, coordinated revisions with the florist, obtained additional vendor timing confirmations, conducted further planning meetings, completed the final venue walkthrough, coordinated the rehearsal, and prepared the final wedding-day schedule. The evidence therefore shows not an absence of service, but an ongoing planning process in which some matters remained subject to client decisions and vendor confirmation and were subsequently addressed.

Independent Vendors
The agreement's Section 6 distinguishes Hana Luxvita's services from the services performed by independent third-party providers. The photographer, florist, and DJ were independent providers responsible for their own contracted services.
Hana Luxvita recognizes that Section 6.5 preserves Hana Luxvita's responsibility for coordination obligations that it expressly undertook. Hana Luxvita does not rely on the independent-provider provisions to avoid its own coordination responsibilities.
The floral issue illustrates the distinction. Sophia objected to an initial floral proposal, and Hana Luxvita worked with the florist to revise it. The final design restored the principal floral elements. The particular blush garden rose was ultimately unavailable, and a premium blush spray rose was substituted.
Sections 4.3 and 4.4 also address service-provider substitutions. Hana Luxvita communicated the availability issue, coordinated the revised design, and obtained Sophia's review before the wedding. The record therefore concerns a communicated vendor-availability substitution rather than a failure by Hana Luxvita to perform the coordination services it expressly undertook. [Attachment R-8; Attachment R-9; Attachment R-10; Attachment R-15]

Planning and Timeline Concerns
Hana Luxvita also disputes that the planning timeline concerns demonstrate a failure to provide the contracted service.
The records show that Sophia raised concerns in July and that Hana Luxvita responded with additional planning information and subsequent timeline updates. The schedule became progressively more complete as vendor confirmations, client decisions, venue access, and other event details were finalized.
Section 7.2 recognizes that customer selections and approvals can affect timing, while Section 7.7 preserves Hana Luxvita's independent obligations. The evidence shows both circumstances here: Sophia had certain information and approvals to provide, while Hana Luxvita continued to coordinate the vendor and planning work.
By the final planning and rehearsal records, the principal vendor timings and wedding-day coordination arrangements had been confirmed before the wedding. The September 20 walkthrough records the confirmed photography, floral, DJ, venue-access, and portrait timings and identifies Hana Luxvita's wedding-day coordination responsibilities. [Attachment R-12; Attachment R-13; Attachment R-14; Attachment R-15]
The fact that some details remained subject to confirmation during the planning process does not establish that Hana Luxvita failed to provide the planning and scheduling services contemplated by Section 15.

Coordinator Change
Hana Luxvita also disputes that the last-minute change in coordinator constituted a failure to perform the agreement.
Maya Ellis became unavailable because of an unexpected family emergency. Hana Luxvita assigned Jordan Lee as the replacement coordinator and provided Jordan with the planning file, vendor confirmations, floral information, photography schedule, DJ details, rehearsal information, and outstanding preferences. Jordan was scheduled to remain on site for the full coordination period and to maintain the master timeline and coordinate the event. [Attachment R-17]
Hana Luxvita acknowledges that Sophia had to repeat some information to Jordan and understands why that was frustrating. However, Hana Luxvita arranged a replacement and continued to provide the wedding-day coordination required under Section 15.

Wedding-Day Coordination
The October 17 activity record shows that Jordan coordinated vendor arrivals, venue access, photography, floral setup, DJ setup, the ceremony, reception transition, and the event timeline. The wedding proceeded without a vendor cancellation or material service interruption. [Attachment R-18]
Sophia identifies one reception-transition incident in which she personally answered a question concerning the order of two reception events.
Hana Luxvita does not dispute that the incident occurred. However, it was an isolated issue that was resolved without material disruption. The reception continued and the planned program proceeded.
The broader wedding-day record shows substantial performance of the coordination services required under Section 15.
Substantial Performance and Corrective Efforts
The agreement's complaint and corrective-action provisions are particularly relevant to the dispute.
Sophia raised concerns before the wedding, and Hana Luxvita was given opportunities to address them. Hana Luxvita responded by revising planning materials, coordinating vendor information, addressing the floral issue, conducting additional planning meetings, completing the venue walkthrough, coordinating the rehearsal, and providing a replacement coordinator.
The evidence therefore shows that Hana Luxvita did not disregard the reported concerns. While Hana Luxvita recognizes that Sophia experienced some inconvenience, the record demonstrates substantial performance of the package and corrective efforts before the wedding.
Sections 9.3 through 9.5 provide for an opportunity to correct issues and make clear that a complaint and attempted correction do not, by themselves, establish whether a breach occurred.
Section 16.6 specifically provides that a payment dispute concerning whether a service was provided or whether a refund is owed is determined under the applicable service, cancellation, refund, and dispute-resolution provisions. Section 17 addresses refund considerations in the context of cancellation, including services performed, services not performed, third-party commitments, and the nature and materiality of an alleged failure. Although Sophia did not cancel the package before the wedding, these provisions are relevant to evaluating the parties' respective positions concerning the disputed payment. [Attachment C-3]

Disputed Card Charge
Sophia does not dispute that she authorized the $3,000 credit-card transaction. The issue is therefore whether Hana Luxvita is entitled to retain that authorized charge based on the services actually provided.
The transaction record identifies the January 15, 2026 online purchase from Hana Luxvita Weddings LLC in the amount of $3,000, including the transaction authorization and reference information. [Attachment C-2]
Hana Luxvita maintains that the charge corresponded to services that were actually performed. The October 23 response identifies planning and coordination, vendor research and coordination, wedding-day coordination, and administrative/finalization work as services performed and calculated the value of those services at $3,000. Hana Luxvita nevertheless offered $500 as a goodwill resolution without admitting that the contracted services were materially deficient. [Attachment R-19]
Hana Luxvita later increased that goodwill offer to $750, expressly recognizing the inconvenience associated with the late coordinator substitution and the transition to the replacement coordinator while maintaining that the evidence did not establish a material failure sufficient to require a larger return of the payment. [Attachment R-20]

Contractual Liability Limitation
Section 10.4 provides that, to the extent permitted by applicable law, Hana Luxvita's aggregate liability arising from a particular purchased service or package will not exceed the amount actually paid by the customer for that service or package. Section 10.6 preserves exceptions where such a limitation is prohibited by applicable law. [Attachment C-3]
Hana Luxvita therefore requests that these provisions be considered if the arbitrator determines that any monetary remedy is appropriate.

Request for Relief
Hana Luxvita recognizes that Sophia experienced frustrations during the planning process and does not dispute that certain matters required additional clarification or coordination. However, the evidence shows substantial performance of the purchased services, corrective efforts before the wedding, completion of the final planning process, and actual wedding-day coordination.
Sophia received the planning and coordination services associated with the disputed transaction. The documented deficiencies do not establish that the entire $3,000 authorized card charge should be returned.

For these reasons, Hana Luxvita respectfully requests that the arbitrator deny Sophia's challenge to the $3,000 charge and permit Hana Luxvita to retain the charge. Alternatively, if the arbitrator determines that some portion of the charge should be returned, Hana Luxvita requests that any adjustment reflect the substantial services actually performed, the corrective efforts made during the planning process, the independent-vendor issues, and the applicable contractual provisions.
