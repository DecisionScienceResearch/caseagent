## Claim (Emily Carter)

I am challenging the permanent termination of my Gen Chorus Voice account after a third content incident. I understand that the platform has rules concerning violent or threatening content, and I do not dispute that the disputed image appeared in my post.

My position is that the image was uploaded by mistake. I was preparing a post about civic engagement and peaceful protest and intended to use a different image. I selected the wrong file from my device and did not realize the mistake immediately. I explained what happened when I appealed the decision and provided information supporting what I had intended to post.

I understand that I had received two earlier warnings, and I am not asking the arbitrator to ignore those warnings. My concern is that the third incident should have been considered in light of what actually happened. The Terms allow the platform to consider whether conduct was intentional or inadvertent, the surrounding circumstances, previous conduct, and information provided during an appeal. The Terms also specifically address situations where a user says the wrong file was uploaded.

I believe permanent termination was too severe in these circumstances. The disputed upload was not an intentional attempt to promote violence, and I had an explanation and supporting information that should have been considered before the account was permanently terminated.

The termination also caused the cancellation of a $24,500 commercial campaign with Pioneer Energy Solutions. I am therefore seeking $24,500 in monetary relief to the extent allowed under the Terms, as well as reinstatement of my account and restoration of access to my account and Content.

I am asking the arbitrator to consider the circumstances of the third incident, my explanation and appeal, and whether permanent termination was an appropriate response.

## Answer (Gen Chorus Voice, Inc.)

Gen Chorus Voice, Inc. maintains that Emily Carter's account was permanently terminated after a third violation of the platform's Community Standards, following two earlier enforcement actions and warnings. The March 1, 2026 incident was therefore considered in the context of the account's prior enforcement history and under the enforcement procedures contained in the Terms.

Emily's account had previously been subject to enforcement on two occasions. The first incident resulted in a warning, and the second incident resulted in another warning. Following the third incident on March 1, 2026, Gen Chorus Voice reviewed the Content and permanently terminated the account on March 2, 2026. The enforcement history records the three incidents, the two prior warnings, and the subsequent termination and appeal.

The Terms do not require each enforcement decision to be considered in isolation. Section 18 permits Gen Chorus Voice to consider the nature and severity of the violation, potential risk of harm, whether the conduct appears intentional or inadvertent, the surrounding circumstances, prior violations, previous warnings, substantially similar conduct, the user's response to prior enforcement, and information submitted during an appeal when selecting an enforcement measure.

Section 19 further provides that, where a user has previously received a warning concerning substantially similar conduct, Gen Chorus Voice may impose a more serious enforcement measure for a subsequent violation. The provision does not make a prior warning conclusive of a later violation; rather, it permits the prior enforcement history to be considered when determining the appropriate response to subsequent conduct.

The Terms also expressly provide for permanent suspension or termination in appropriate circumstances. Section 21 identifies repeated violations following previous warnings or enforcement actions, and repeated substantially similar violations, among the circumstances that may support permanent suspension or termination.

Emily states that the March 1 image was uploaded accidentally because she selected the wrong file while preparing a post concerning civic engagement and peaceful protest. Gen Chorus Voice considered that explanation under Section 22, which specifically addresses inadvertent uploads. Section 22 permits consideration of the intended Content, the difference between the intended and uploaded Content, the circumstances surrounding the upload, the user's explanation, other contemporaneous Content, prior conduct, and other reasonably available evidence. It also makes clear that an assertion of accidental upload does not automatically establish that no violation occurred or require restoration of an account.

The accidental-upload explanation therefore did not automatically resolve the enforcement issue. The relevant question was whether, after considering that explanation together with the March 1 Content, the prior violations and warnings, and the other circumstances identified in the Terms, the appropriate enforcement measure was permanent termination.

Emily also appealed the enforcement decision. The appeal process permits a user to provide additional factual context, evidence concerning an alleged inadvertent upload, and an explanation of the intended meaning of the Content. The appeal process does not, however, require restoration of an account simply because an appeal is submitted. Gen Chorus Voice reviewed Emily's explanation and the available information before maintaining the enforcement decision.

The resulting enforcement record reflects that the March 1 incident was evaluated together with the prior enforcement history. The appeal was reviewed and denied on March 2, 2026, and the termination was maintained. The record therefore does not support the proposition that Emily's explanation was ignored; rather, it shows that the explanation was considered but did not result in reversal of the enforcement decision.

The $24,500 monetary claim should also be denied. The Pioneer Energy Solutions agreement identifies $36,750 in total campaign compensation, including a $12,250 upfront payment and a remaining $24,500 balance payable after completion and approval of the required deliverables. The $24,500 therefore was not an amount that had already become unconditionally payable when the account was terminated.

In addition, Section 44 of the Terms limits liability, to the maximum extent permitted by applicable law, for indirect, incidental, special, consequential, exemplary, and punitive damages, expressly including lost profits, lost revenue, lost business opportunities, and anticipated commercial benefits. Section 44 also provides that Gen Chorus Voice will not be responsible for losses resulting solely from interruption, suspension, or termination of an account where the action was taken in accordance with the Terms.

Section 46 separately provides that a user's loss of sponsorships, advertising opportunities, followers, audience engagement, or other commercial benefits following an account restriction or termination does not, by itself, establish liability.

Gen Chorus Voice does not contend that these provisions prevent the arbitrator from determining whether the termination complied with the Terms. Section 45 expressly preserves that determination and also recognizes that liability cannot be excluded to the extent applicable law prohibits such a limitation. Rather, Gen Chorus Voice maintains that the contractual limitations apply to the claimed commercial loss to the extent permitted by applicable law.

For these reasons, Gen Chorus Voice maintains that the March 2, 2026 termination was authorized by and consistent with the enforcement framework in the Terms, that Emily's inadvertent-upload explanation did not require reversal of the decision, and that the claimed $24,500 commercial loss is not recoverable under the applicable contractual limitations and, independently, was not an amount already earned or presently payable.

Gen Chorus Voice therefore respectfully requests that the arbitrator uphold the termination, deny reinstatement of the account, and deny the $24,500 monetary claim.

## Response (Gen Chorus Voice, Inc.)

Gen Chorus Voice acknowledges Emily Carter's explanation that the March 1, 2026 image was uploaded unintentionally. The platform does not contend that an inadvertent-upload explanation is irrelevant. The Terms expressly permit that circumstance to be considered. The disagreement is over what consequence that explanation should have.

Section 22 does not provide that an accidental-upload explanation requires a violation to be disregarded or an account to be restored. It provides factors that may be considered when evaluating the circumstances, including the intended Content, the difference between the intended and uploaded Content, the circumstances of the upload, the user's explanation, contemporaneous Content, prior conduct, and other reasonably available evidence.

Gen Chorus Voice considered those circumstances together with Emily's enforcement history. The issue was therefore not whether Emily was permitted to explain what happened; she was. The issue was whether that explanation outweighed the seriousness of the March 1 violation and the account's previous enforcement history. The Terms permit the platform to consider both.

Emily correctly notes that Section 19 does not make a prior warning conclusive of a later violation. Gen Chorus Voice is not arguing otherwise. The March 1 Content was separately evaluated as a violation. The prior warnings were relevant to the appropriate enforcement measure, rather than being treated as proof that the March 1 Content violated the rules. Section 19 expressly permits a more serious enforcement measure after a substantially similar subsequent violation.

Emily also argues that Section 21 does not make permanent termination automatic. Gen Chorus Voice agrees that termination is not mechanically required in every case. Section 21 instead identifies circumstances that may support permanent termination, including repeated violations following previous warnings or enforcement actions and repeated substantially similar violations. Those circumstances were present here and were considered together with the March 1 incident.

The fact that the arbitrator may independently determine whether the enforcement action complied with the Terms does not mean that the arbitrator should substitute a different enforcement decision merely because another outcome might also have been possible. The contractual framework gave Gen Chorus Voice discretion to evaluate the relevant circumstances and select an appropriate enforcement measure. The record shows that the March 1 incident, prior warnings, and Emily's appeal were reviewed before the termination was maintained.

The appeal record also confirms that Emily's explanation and supporting materials were reviewed before the termination was maintained. Gen Chorus Voice therefore does not contend that Emily's explanation was ignored. Rather, the platform's position is that the explanation was considered together with the nature of the March 1 Content and the account's prior enforcement history, and that the circumstances as a whole supported maintaining the enforcement decision.

The damages claim presents a separate problem. Emily characterizes the $24,500 as a commercial loss caused by the termination. But the Pioneer agreement establishes that the $24,500 was the remaining balance of a $36,750 campaign, payable after completion and approval of the required deliverables. It was therefore not an amount that Emily had already received or was unconditionally entitled to receive at the time her account was terminated.

The Pioneer cancellation evidence may establish that the campaign was cancelled following the account termination, but it does not eliminate the contractual limitations governing Gen Chorus Voice's liability. The cancellation of a commercial opportunity is the type of loss addressed by Section 44, which limits liability, to the maximum extent permitted by applicable law, for lost profits, lost revenue, lost business opportunities, and anticipated commercial benefits. Section 44 also addresses losses resulting solely from interruption, suspension, or termination of an account where the action was taken in accordance with the Terms.

Section 46 further provides that the loss of sponsorships, advertising opportunities, or other commercial benefits following an account restriction or termination does not, by itself, establish liability. Emily's evidence may establish that a commercial opportunity was lost, but the existence of that loss does not overcome the contractual limitation.

Gen Chorus Voice recognizes, as Emily points out, that Section 45 preserves the arbitrator's authority to determine whether the enforcement action complied with the Terms and preserves liability that cannot lawfully be excluded. Gen Chorus Voice therefore does not ask the arbitrator to treat Section 44 as eliminating the arbitrator's review of the termination. Rather, the platform asks the arbitrator to apply the contractual limitations to the claimed commercial loss to the extent permitted by the Terms and applicable law.

Finally, Emily's explanation and supporting evidence do not eliminate the significance of the prior warnings. The platform's position is not that Emily's mistake was irrelevant. It is that, after considering the mistake, the March 1 incident remained the third enforcement event following two prior warnings, and the Terms permitted Gen Chorus Voice to consider that history when determining the appropriate enforcement measure.

For these reasons, Gen Chorus Voice maintains that the permanent termination was within the enforcement framework established by the Terms and that the $24,500 commercial-loss claim is subject to the contractual limitations in the Terms, to the extent permitted by applicable law.

Gen Chorus Voice therefore respectfully requests that the arbitrator uphold the termination, deny reinstatement, and deny the requested $24,500 monetary relief.
