# Inventory Consignment Dispute: the sample's own arguments

Reference only. These are the human-written arguments that ship with the DecisionLayer sample. caseagent never reads this file: every filing it produces is written by the agent from the dispute, the contract and the exhibits.

## Claim (Verdant Micro Inc.)

From our perspective as a business partner, this is a clear-cut issue of operational compliance and contract breach. We delivered a major shipment of network switches and encryption modules to the Harrisburg facility. Teletronics accepted the inventory, found no issues with it, and successfully sold the units to their customer base, collecting a total of $143,000.00 in gross revenue.  The agreement we signed is explicit about how this money must be handled. Section 9.2 states that Teletronics must forward all amounts received from customers to us completely intact and "without deduction." The contract does not permit them to net out their commission or hold the cash. Our agreement dictates a strict sequence: we get our gross sales revenue first, and then we calculate and pay out their 8% commission the following month under Section 10.2. By keeping the entire $143,000.00 to fund their own operational expenses, Teletronics has completely bypassed the contract and treated our customer revenue as their own private cash reserve.

## Answer (Teletronics Inc.)

We value our relationship with Verdant, and we aren't disputing that we collected $143,000.00 from the end customers. However, the severe liquidity crunch in the telecom hardware sector at the start of 2026 put incredible strain on our working capital, forcing us to look closely at the commercial fairness of this cash pipeline.  The payment terms in this agreement are structurally lopsided and place all the financial strain on the distributor. Verdant expects us to instantly route gross customer revenues back to them without touching a penny, while they get to sit on our earned 8% commissions for weeks before paying them out under Section 10.2. In a tight market, forcing us to send away gross cash while waiting on a lagging commission payment creates an unsustainable cash-flow drain on our local operations. We fully intend to pay the $143,000.00, but we are asking for a practical, 120-day payout plan that allows us to clear the balance safely without breaking our local distribution network

## Reply (Verdant Micro Inc.)

Teletronics’ argument actually highlights exactly why we wrote the contract the way we did. The "without deduction" rule exists specifically so that a distributor cannot use our gross product revenues as a temporary shield against their own localized liquidity problems.  Their complaint about the commission timeline under Section 10.2 completely misrepresents the situation. They aren't just delaying the gross funds while waiting for a commission adjustment—they have withheld the entire $143,000.00 gross balance, meaning they have frozen both our principal capital and the sales revenue. A company cannot use an industry downturn to rewrite a sequential payment structure on the fly. We are entitled to the full gross amount immediately, and their commissions can only be processed through the proper contractual channels once that principal is safely in our accounts.

## Response (Teletronics Inc.)

We completely reject the accusation that we are using Verdant’s capital as a private cash reserve or acting in bad faith. The decision to temporarily hold the $143,000.00 in gross proceeds is a direct response to a major commercial failure on Verdant's side regarding the SecureWave Encryption Modules. While we did sell the units, the product integration was plagued by critical firmware bugs that forced our regional engineering team to spend dozens of uncompensated hours handling emergency customer service and manual patch deployments just to save the accounts.  Under Section 8.2 of the agreement, Verdant is explicitly obligated to provide the marketing material, info, and technical support necessary to properly sustain these goods in the market. By delivering a faulty product rollout that required extensive, costly technical salvage from our local team, Verdant breached its supporting obligations. We are holding the $143,000.00 secure in our accounts not to escape the debt, but as legitimate commercial leverage to ensure Verdant negotiates a fair operational credit for the extraordinary support costs we incurred on their behalf. We maintain that the only equitable resolution is a commercial settlement that deducts our documented field-support expenses from the gross balance before any final cash is routed.
